Part E · AI and AR21. How the AI works

In plain words. When the user points the phone at a product, the phone itself reads the words on the front and any barcode, many times a second, and throws away blurry or dark pictures. It compares what it read with its own copy of the GlowUp catalogue. If the product is there, its stored ingredient list is used at once, even offline. If not, the app asks the database by brand and name, then (online, a limited number of times) asks Google's Gemini AI to recognise the product from one picture and find its official ingredient list on the web with a source page. If that also fails, the user can take one photo for an admin to approve, choose from a shortlist, or type the product in. The ingredient check against the user's skin profile is always done on the phone and is never sent to the AI. {V:Scripts/Scan/SmartScanFlowController.cs header and lines 749-826, 1060-1249; Scripts/Scan/ScanAnalysisPipeline.cs header}

Part E · AI and AR22. Recommendation logic

In plain words. The app never sends the profile anywhere to get recommendations. It takes every make-up product in its catalogue copy and analyses its ingredients for this user, exactly like a scan. A product is suggested only if nothing is flagged. For a user who saved a chronic skin condition, the bar is higher: the ingredient list must come from a trusted source and every ingredient must be known. Skin-care products can never be suggested, because the catalogue and the rule are make-up only. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductRecommender.cs lines 1-108}
