Part A · Start here1. Project summary

The problem. People buy make-up without understanding its ingredient list, and someone with sensitive skin, an allergy or a chronic skin condition cannot easily tell whether a product may cause a reaction. Shared testers in shops are also a hygiene concern, so trying a shade before buying is hard. {I:docs/GlowUp_Methodology_Replacement.pdf p.19, "survey of 502 residents ... hygiene of testers, ingredient understanding, shade matching"}

The solution. GlowUp is an Android app that recognises a make-up product with the phone camera (or by typing its name), finds its ingredient list, and compares every ingredient with the user's own skin profile: allergens, skin type, skin conditions and age. It shows a simple verdict (safe, caution, harmful), explains why, suggests make-up that has nothing flagged for that person, and lets the user try the product's shades on their own face with augmented reality. {V:Scripts/Scan/ScanAnalysisPipeline.cs, Scripts/Scan/ProductAnalysisEngine.cs, Scripts/Scan/ProductRecommender.cs, Scripts/AR/ArTryOnFlowController.cs}

Who it is for. End users (app) who wear make-up, and administrators (website) who keep the shared brand, product and ingredient database correct. Both use one Firebase project and one Firestore database. {V:GlowUp-Admin_AI_Testing/FIRESTORE_SCHEMA.md, GlowUp-Admin_AI_Testing/.firebaserc}

What it is not. It is not a medical tool: every result says it is general information, and the app recommends seeing a dermatologist when something is flagged. It checks make-up only, never skin care, hair, body, nail or fragrance products. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductScope.cs, Legal/PrivacyPolicyAndTerms.txt §4 Terms, Scripts/Scan/ProductAnalysisEngine.cs line 209}

2.2 Scope

  • Make-up only. 17 make-up kinds (lipstick, lip gloss, lip tint, lip liner, foundation, concealer, primer, powder, blush, bronzer, highlighter, contour, eyeshadow, eyeliner, mascara, brow, setting spray). Skin care and hair/body/nail/fragrance are refused; unclear kinds (tinted moisturizer, BB/CC cream, lip balm, lip oil, tinted sunscreen) are held as Pending. The same rule is written in C# and JavaScript and tested against the same example files. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductScope.cs, GlowUp-Admin_AI_Testing/_Shared/glowupRules.js, Editor/Tests/MakeupScopeCases.txt, Editor/Tests/SharedRuleCases.txt}
  • Platform. Android phones. The build tooling targets Android only (IL2CPP, ARMv7 + ARM64, OpenGL ES 3, minimum API 23). {V:Editor/ArFaceTrackingSetup.cs lines 99-147} An iOS build is not configured. {I:no iOS build settings in _Project/Editor; only vendor iOS files of the Facebook SDK exist}
  • Region. The Philippines: products are checked against the FDA Philippines verification portal, and the AI look-up gives the local market's rules priority. {V:Scripts/Scan/PhFdaVerification.cs, Scripts/Scan/IngredientResearcher.cs}

2.4 Limitations

AreaLimitation
Offline useRecognition of catalogue products and the ingredient check work offline for ingredients the phone knows. The AI identification, web list look-up, FDA Philippines check, Open Beauty Facts and the photo snapshot need the internet. {V:Scripts/Scan/ScanAnalysisPipeline.cs header, Scripts/Scan/ProductAiAdvisor.cs "ONLINE ONLY"}
LightingFrames that are too dark, glared, moving or blurred are thrown away (thresholds in Scan Rules). A flashlight can be switched on. AR face tracking struggles in the dark; a low-light hint and a white "screen light" frame help. {V:Scripts/Scan/FrameQuality.cs, Scripts/Scan/ScanRules.cs, Scripts/AR/ArLightAdapter.cs, Scripts/AR/ScreenLightFrame.cs}
Ingredient lists from the cameraIn the current branch the live scan reads only the front label and barcode; ingredient lists come from the catalogue, the web look-up, the photo snapshot or typed text, not from OCR of the pack's small print (captureIngredients is passed as false). {V:Scripts/Scan/SmartScanFlowController.cs line 786, Scripts/Scan/ScanEvidence.cs lines 112-133, 201-202}
Product coverageThe catalogue is small: about 700 documents across the three lists. {V:Scripts/Data/CatalogSync.cs header} One import added 278 make-up products of 38 brands from the owner's brand list. {I:commit 9188bc2 message} An unknown product needs internet and an admin's approval before it is recommended.
Ingredient coverageAn ingredient the app does not know is "not checked" and never counted as safe; with too many of them the result is "incomplete". {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductAnalysisEngine.cs lines 59-64, 143-184} But an ingredient entry whose riskLevel is empty is treated as "safe" unless the built-in table knows better. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductAnalysisEngine.cs line 72, GlowUp-Admin_AI_Testing/IMPORT_FORMAT.md}
Shade accuracySuggested shades come from a fixed table of six colours per skin tone and face area; AR colour depends on the screen, camera and light. {V:Scripts/Scan/ShadeAdvisor.cs, Legal/PrivacyPolicyAndTerms.txt Terms §5}
Device supportLive AR needs ARCore face tracking and a 64-bit build; other phones get the guided preview. Finger signs and the pack-look model need Android 7.0+. {V:Scripts/AR/ArFaceTrackingController.cs, Scripts/Common/HandLandmarkReader.cs, Scripts/Scan/PackEmbedder.cs}
Accuracy figuresNo measured recognition accuracy, OCR accuracy or AR frame rate on a phone is recorded in the repository. The only measured figures are desktop checks of the pack-look model (94% found at 0.55 with 1.6% wrong; 68% at 0.70 with 0.4% wrong, on 76 public photos). {V:Scripts/Scan/PackagingMemory.cs header} {N:phone-measured recognition accuracy, OCR accuracy, AR fps}
AI quotaThe AI is limited per scan visit (2 recognitions, 2 list look-ups by default) and the photo function to 5 per account and 150 for everybody per day. {V:Scripts/Scan/ScanRules.cs lines 40-42, GlowUp-Admin_AI_Testing/api/scan-snapshot.js lines 17-19}
Medical scopeInformational only; never a diagnosis or a treatment. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductScope.cs header, Legal/PrivacyPolicyAndTerms.txt}
