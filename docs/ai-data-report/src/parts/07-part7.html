Part 7 · Shade data and true colour (belief B4)21a. Shade codes versus colours

The team's shade workflow (core idea)

Make-up brands label shades with codes such as "131" or "12A", not with colour values. When a user scans a product, the AI cannot read an actual colour from such a code. So the admin (1) records the shade code exactly as printed, (2) looks up the real colour and enters its hex code, and (3) the system links code to hex to the AR render.

23. Honest accuracy statement

A hex value is a reference colour, not a guarantee of what the user sees. What changes the colour on screen:

  • The phone's display: calibration, brightness, night-light / blue-light filters.
  • The room: warm lamps, cool daylight, dim light; the selfie camera's own white balance and exposure.
  • The user's skin and lips: the colour is laid over the camera picture, so a sheer shade on deep lips looks deeper than on pale lips, as in real life. The AR shader multiplies the picture by the ratio of shade to an assumed skin colour; it can at most double the brightness of a pixel, so a very light shade on very deep lips cannot be fully reached. {V:Shaders/FaceMakeupBlend.shader lines 8-10, 63-66}
  • Opacity and finish: matte, satin, gloss and shimmer reflect light differently.

What the team can truthfully claim: "AR Try-On shows each shade consistently and faithful to the reference colour the admin entered, adapted to the user's lighting and skin. It is a preview, not an exact colour match; the real product can look different on your skin and screen." Do not claim "the real colour of the product" or "exact colour".

24. Getting shade data right (admin guidance)

  1. Best source: a colour value the manufacturer publishes (rare). Otherwise:
  2. Swatch photo under neutral light: swatch the product on white card next to a grey/white reference card, daylight-balanced light (about 5000-6500 K), no filters; take the hex from the middle of the swatch after setting the white balance on the reference card.
  3. Colour reader (a cheap colourimeter) gives the most repeatable value.
  4. Never take the hex from a retailer's product photo of the tube (packaging colour, retouching) and never from the box.
