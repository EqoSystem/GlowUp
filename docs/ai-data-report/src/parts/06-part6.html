20. Why the AI does not match scans with catalogue images (belief B3)

The team's reason (primary)

A product in a user's hand often looks different from its catalogue picture: worn packaging, incomplete or scratched letters, faded printing. That makes picture matching uncertain. The team's priority is a fast result, so the main goal is to avoid latency. Picture-based recognition needs heavier work (a model on each picture and a comparison against many reference pictures) and behaves unpredictably on worn products, which hurts both speed and reliability.

What the code actually does

  1. Text and barcode first, on the phone. Barcode and name look-ups are in-memory dictionary steps over the phone's copy. {V:Scripts/Data/ProductIndex.cs header}
  2. A small picture model does run on the phone, but it compares a scan only with packs this phone scanned before, never with catalogue images, and it only asks "Is this ...?". {V:Scripts/Scan/PackagingMemory.cs; Scripts/Scan/PackEmbedder.cs; commit 50e5558}
  3. The online AI does see a picture of the scan (one JPEG) when the phone cannot name the product. {V:Scripts/Scan/ProductAiAdvisor.cs line 165}
  4. Catalogue images are not used anywhere in recognition. The app never downloads them. {V:grep of Scripts/ for imageUrl: no hit}

So "the AI cannot use product images" is not accurate. The accurate statement: the app does not use catalogue images for recognition, by design.

Evidence for the team's reasoning

  • The old colour-and-pattern signature found the same pack again in about 1 of 100 desktop tries; the picture model in 94 of 100 at the medium limit, with 16 wrong matches in 1000 from packs of one product line that really look alike. That is why the app asks and never decides by the look alone. {V:commit 50e5558 message}
  • A desktop check of the same picture model on 24 made-up brand word marks put the right brand first in 67% of cases with the same colours, 41% on another background and 21% light-on-dark, with no limit that separated right from wrong marks. {I:developer's desktop check of 13 October 2026, recorded outside the repository}
  • There is no vector search in Firestore; matching many reference images would need a separate index. {V:FIRESTORE_SCHEMA.md line 121}

Why catalogue images are a poor reference

Studio photo vs real-world scan (angle, glare, background), wear and scratches, regional packaging, batch and redesign changes, products that differ only by shade, and hundreds of near-identical shades in one line. Building it properly would need a labelled dataset per product, an embedding index, a larger model on the phone, and per-shade confusion handling.
