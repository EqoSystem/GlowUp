37. How the colour blends (the math, not a phone photo)

No phone or ARCore-capable emulator was available, so there are no screenshots of the try-on on a face. {N:before/after screenshots of AR Try-On on a phone} Instead, the table below applies the shader's own formula (the C# copy ArColorMath.Tint, tested) in linear light to bare lips of different depths. The lips' own light is kept, so the same lipstick looks deeper on deeper lips, as in real life; very light shades on very deep lips stop at double the brightness.

Bare lipsT01 Classic red #B3202ET03 Warm nude #B9786AT04 Pale pink #F2A7B8T02 Cool berry #7E2448

{V:Scripts/AR/ArColorMath.cs; Shaders/FaceMakeupBlend.shader} The bare-lip colours are illustrative values, not measured from people.

Known limit: pale shades on deep lips

Look at the pale pink column: on the two deepest lips it stays brown. A real opaque pale lipstick covers dark lips; the tint blend can only multiply the picture (at most x2), so it behaves like a sheer balm. This is a fairness issue for users with deep lips and must be checked on real phones. Proposed fix (not built): a second, light pigment pass whose strength follows the product's coverage (proposed shade field, Section 24), mixing in at most about a third of the shade colour scaled by the lips' own brightness, so texture stays visible. It needs tuning on several skin tones, so it was not added without a phone.
